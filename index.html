✨ 7.0分 範文參考 (Rewritten Version)：

The tables illustrate the population changes in New York City and the distribution of residents across its five districts between 1800 and 2000.

Overall, it is evident that the total population of New York City skyrocketed over the two centuries. Furthermore, there was a dramatic shift in demographics; while Manhattan housed the vast majority of residents in 1800, the other districts accounted for the bulk of the population by 2000.

Looking at the total numbers, New York City had a population of 79,216 in 1800. This figure surged to nearly 3.5 million in 1900 and continued to rise, reaching a peak of 8 million in 2000. A similar upward trend was seen in the outer districts (Brooklyn, Bronx, Queens, and Staten Island), where the population grew from a mere 18,701 in 1800 to almost 6.5 million in 2000.

Regarding the percentage distribution, Manhattan started as the dominant district, hosting 76% of the city's total population in 1800. However, this share dropped significantly to 54% in 1900 and plummeted further to just 19% by 2000. In stark contrast, the proportion of residents living in the other four districts rose steadily, starting at 24% and climbing to 81% by the end of the period.
